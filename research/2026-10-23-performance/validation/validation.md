Other-ecosystem claims are confirmed, with one useful update:

- [Python Unidecode 1.4.0](https://pypi.org/project/Unidecode/) is GPL-2.0-or-later and documents unknown-character policies and separate ASCII/non-ASCII-oriented entry points. Its own claimed speed ratios were not reused as benchmark results.
- [text-unidecode](https://github.com/kmike/text-unidecode) is a simpler port with Artistic/GPL choices. **Update python-slugify:** current [PyPI documentation](https://pypi.org/project/python-slugify/) covers the 9.x line, explicit text-unidecode/Unidecode/AnyASCII backends and legacy versus modern algorithms. Legacy remains default; auto prefers installed Unidecode, otherwise text-unidecode. The AnyASCII extra does not remove the base text-unidecode dependency. Unicode preservation, length, stopwords and word boundaries remain supported; wrapper and backend licenses are separate.
- [Rust deunicode](https://github.com/kornelski/deunicode) documents compact static data and context-free limits. Its [source](https://github.com/kornelski/deunicode/blob/main/src/lib.rs) exposes per-character and iterator interfaces plus a Cow-returning path that can avoid allocation. This supports architectural comparison; it does not establish performance through Node FFI/WASM.
- [AnyAscii](https://github.com/anyascii/anyascii) documents matching implementations across multiple languages. [go-unidecode](https://github.com/mozillazg/go-unidecode) is another Unicode-to-ASCII table implementation; neither was timed in this validation.
- [ICU transforms](https://unicode-org.github.io/icu/userguide/transforms/general/) explicitly support context-sensitive rules and compound transforms. That capability differs from a one-code-point table and does not imply full pronunciation recovery.
